# Empire Cooler Corporate Partners Project

In Fall 2025, I worked on a Corporate Partners project with Empire Cooler through Purdue University’s Data Mine learning community. As part of this collaboration, I worked in an Agile team environment where tasks were organized weekly and distributed among team members. This project focused on building the foundations of an AI-powered system designed to help refrigeration technicians quickly access accurate, model-specific repair information. The system aimed to modernize how technicians search through decades of manuals, service notes, and online resources.

## Empire Cooler

Empire Cooler is a Chicago-based commercial refrigeration and ice machine service company with over 90 years of industry experience. Their technicians service thousands of restaurants, bars, and commercial kitchens, maintaining a wide range of refrigeration and ice machine models.

Because refrigeration systems vary significantly by brand, model, and year, critical repair information is often scattered across manuals, service bulletins, forums, and historical documentation. This makes diagnosing issues time-consuming, especially in the field where technicians need fast and precise answers. Empire Cooler partnered with The Data Mine to explore how AI and data-driven systems could improve diagnostic workflows and technician efficiency.

## The Project

This project involved working with large volumes of technical documentation, including manufacturer manuals, service notes, and online troubleshooting discussions. Due to confidentiality requirements, specific proprietary details cannot be shared; however, the general scope and nature of my work can be described.

The primary objective was to transform unstructured and inconsistent documentation into a structured, searchable knowledge base that could support an AI-powered diagnostic assistant. The project followed an Agile workflow, with tasks organized and tracked weekly rather than focusing strictly on fixed sprint deliverables.

## My Contributions

Over the course of the semester, I completed a variety of tasks, including:

- Built Python scripts to collect and clean large amounts of unstructured data from manufacturer manuals, technician blogs, and online forums.
- Helped process and organize extracted text into smaller, meaningful sections suitable for efficient search and retrieval.
- Added metadata to documents to improve the relevance and accuracy of search results.
- Assisted in testing and refining a prototype system designed to surface accurate repair information quickly.
- Collaborated with teammates to evaluate system reliability and usability from a technician’s perspective.

## Technologies Utilized

The project was developed using a remote development environment and collaborative tools. Technologies used include:

- Python  
- Jupyter Notebook
- Visual Studio Code  
- Web scraping and data cleaning tools  
- Text processing and organization techniques  
- Agile project management tools  

## Results

This project emphasized building software foundations rather than traditional data analysis. Through this experience, I gained hands-on exposure to how real-world engineering teams handle unstructured data, collaborate in Agile environments, and design systems with end users in mind.

Working with mentors and teammates provided valuable insight into how AI-driven tools can be responsibly designed to support technical professionals. The project strengthened my understanding of building practical, data-driven systems that address real operational challenges.
